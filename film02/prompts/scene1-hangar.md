A 16-second live-action launch film for a personal electric aircraft, 16:9. One pilot, one aircraft, one hangar at night. The overhead lights wake the aircraft, the pilot suits up, the motors start and the aircraft leaves the hangar over the camera.

CAST — use this same person throughout the clip.
Pilot: an East Asian man around 30, short black hair, light natural stubble, calm focused eyes, real skin with visible pores and natural asymmetry, no beauty retouching. Matte black one-piece flight suit, black leather gloves, a matte black full-face helmet with a dark smoked visor. Quiet, precise, unhurried. Once the visor is down at 5.6s it stays down for the rest of the clip.

AIRCRAFT — exactly one CoolFly Dream single-seat electric multirotor, identical to the references in every frame: an orange glossy front fairing with two white vertical racing stripes and the number "05", a dark-grey tubular steel roll cage with an arched roof, a white bucket seat with harness, four carbon-fibre arms in an X layout, and at the tip of each arm a coaxial pair of black motors, one above and one below, each with a two-blade carbon propeller, eight propellers in total. Open perforated steel floor under the seat. Never redesigned, no extra or missing arms, motors or propellers, no logos or readable text added.

HANGAR — a large dark aircraft hangar at night. Polished concrete floor with a thin film of standing water that mirrors the lights. Six rows of linear LED ceiling fixtures run from the far end toward the camera. The aircraft stands on a painted square pad in the centre, nose toward a tall rolling door at the far end. A steel tool cart stands at the left wall; the pilot enters from a side door at screen right. Behind the rolling door is a pale blue dawn sky over the sea. Keep the pad, cart, side door and rolling door in these physical positions as the camera moves.

LOOK — clean modern digital cinema, 24–85mm lenses, crisp detail, deep blacks, high contrast. Cold white practical light from the ceiling rows; the only strong colour is the aircraft's orange. Fine restrained grain. Physically plausible, weighted camera moves with slow starts and precise stops. No faded grade, artificial glow, lens flares or visible CGI.

SHOTS
0.0–2.0s: Locked 24mm wide from floor level behind the tool cart, looking across the pad toward the rolling door. Total darkness, then the ceiling rows switch on one at a time from the far door toward the camera at 0.3s, 0.6s, 0.9s, 1.2s, 1.5s and 1.8s. Each new row slides a long white highlight further along the orange fairing and lights a new stretch of the wet floor. The aircraft does not move.

2.0–4.0s: Hard cut to a 50mm medium shot at chest height. The pilot walks in from the side door at screen right, helmet carried in his left hand, face fully visible, and stops beside the cockpit at 3.4s. The camera trucks left with him at walking speed and settles. The lit roll cage and the orange fairing fill the left half of frame.

4.0–6.2s: 85mm close-up, slightly low. He lifts the helmet with both gloved hands and pulls it on at 4.6s, settles it, fastens the chin strap with a short tug, then lowers the dark visor with one gloved finger at 5.6s. The visor click is the cut point. The dark visor surface fills most of the frame on the last frame.

6.2–8.0s: Hard cut to a 100mm macro of his right gloved hand closing on the flight control grip inside the cockpit; the carbon arm and a coaxial motor pair are soft in the background. His fingers tighten at 6.8s. At 7.2s the green status lights on the motors blink on one after another, left to right.

8.0–10.5s: Low 24mm at floor level beside one arm tip, the coaxial motors in the right third. At 8.3s both propellers start turning; by 9.2s they are translucent spinning discs. The water film on the floor shivers, then at 9.5s sprays outward in a ring of fine mist toward the lens. Camera locked.

10.5–13.0s: Wide 24mm from the floor near the open rolling door, looking back into the hangar. The aircraft with the seated pilot lifts vertically from the pad to about 1.5 metres between 10.8s and 12.4s, a ring of mist spreading across the wet floor, ceiling lights sliding over the fairing. The camera rises slowly with it and eases to a stop.

13.0–16.0s: Hard cut outside the hangar to ground level just past the rolling door, looking back inside. The aircraft accelerates toward the camera through the door and passes directly over the lens. Its belly, open steel floor and arms fill the whole frame from 15.4s to 16.0s with strong motion blur. End on the covered frame; this is the transition into the next scene.

SOUND — dry, close, natural sound only. Hangar room tone; a heavy electrical clunk for each ceiling row; footsteps on wet concrete; helmet and strap sounds; a crisp visor click at 5.6s; a soft electronic chirp at 7.2s; motor whine rising from 8.3s; water hiss at 9.5s; deep rotor roar from 10.8s; a single huge pass-by roar peaking at 15.6s and cut off at 16.0s. No music, no dialogue, no narrator, no captions or watermarks.

CONTINUITY — exactly one aircraft and one pilot; the aircraft is identical to the references in every shot; the pilot's face is only seen before 4.6s, the helmet stays on and the visor stays down after 5.6s; pad, tool cart, side door and rolling door keep their physical positions; the rolling door is open from 10.5s; no extra people, no readable text.
